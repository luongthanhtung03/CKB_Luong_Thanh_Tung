## What the two funding sources actually are

There are two separate pots of money in the CKB ecosystem. They are run by different
people and they decide in completely different ways.

### Spark — small, fast, decided by a committee

The **Spark Program** is run by the **CKB Eco Fund**, a team inside the ecosystem.
There is no vote. A committee of a few people reads the application and answers.

- **Amount:** up to **$1,000** per project. Exceptional cases up to **$2,000**.
- **Decision:** the Eco Fund evaluation committee, in about **one week**.
- **What it funds:** prototypes, proofs of concept, early user testing — work of
  roughly **1–2 months**. Not finished products.
- **What it will not fund:** financial speculation, or copies of things that already
  exist elsewhere in the industry.
- **Requirements:** a GitHub repository **under my own account**, and an
  **open-source licence — mandatory**.
- **Payment:** 20% on starting, 80% on completion.
- **Where to apply:** the Spark sub-section of the Nervos Talk forum.
- **What they say they are looking for:** "Web5" — Web2 and Web3 combined naturally,
  rather than crypto for its own sake.

### Community Fund DAO — larger, slower, decided by a public vote

The **Community Fund DAO** is the community treasury: **276,000,000 CKB**,
contributed jointly by the community and the Nervos Foundation. **DAO** here means
the *community* decides how the money is spent, by voting, instead of a manager
deciding.

This is the one where I post a proposal, say how much I want, and people vote.

It runs in three stages:

| Stage | Where | How long | What has to happen |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. **`[DIS]`** discussion | Nervos Talk forum | 7 days | The post needs **30 likes** to advance |
| 2. **`[VOT]`** vote | Metaforo | 7 days | **Quorum: 3× the CKB I asked for.** **Approval: ≥51%** of votes cast |
| 3. Execution | — | ~1 week to pay | Progress posted as `[Status Update]` |

- **Title format:** `[DIS] <title>`, then `[VOT] <the same title>`.
- **The `[DIS]` post must answer:** Why do this? Why me? Why now? What is the budget
  and why? How, with milestones?
- **Voting weight is token-weighted** — a voter's weight is how much CKB they hold in
  the Nervos DAO.
- **Over $10,000**, the first payment is capped at 20% (max $10,000) and the rest is
  released against milestones with community progress reviews.
- **Language:** English or Chinese, translation recommended.

### The gate I have to open first

> To start a vote, the proposer must hold **at least 100,000 CKB in the Nervos DAO.**

At roughly **$0.0008 per CKB that is about $80.** It is the cheapest unblock in the
entire twelve weeks, and the Nervos DAO works on ~30-day epoch cycles, so it gets
deposited in **Week 1.5** and left alone — not bought in November when I need it.

*(Note: the "Nervos DAO" here is the protocol's built-in savings mechanism where CKB
is locked to earn interest. It is a different thing from the "Community Fund DAO",
which is the grant treasury. Confusingly similar names; they are unrelated.)*

## The rule that makes two asks per project honest

Every project applies to **both** Spark and the Community Fund DAO. That is only
legitimate if the two asks fund **different work**:

- **Spark = phase 1.** The prototype. Small, already built or nearly built.
- **DAO = phase 2.** What comes after the prototype — mainnet deployment, an audit,
  maintenance, adoption work, operating costs.

**Both applications for a project get written in the same sitting**, so the boundary
is deliberate rather than reconstructed afterwards. The DAO proposal **links the
Spark completion report** as evidence that the previous tranche was actually
delivered.

**If I cannot describe a genuine phase 2 for a project, it does not get a DAO
campaign.** It takes the Spark grant and stops there. Three funding asks in six weeks
from one person can read as farming the treasury, and the only defence is that each
one visibly buys something the last one did not.

## The campaign calendar

One campaign is live at a time. Never two. This falls out naturally because the three
projects finish at different times — but if a project slips, **its campaign slips
too**. Do not compress the gap to catch up.

| Project | Spark submitted | `[DIS]` (7d) | `[VOT]` (7d) | Result |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **P3** cycle tools | Wk 8 · 2–7 Nov | 9–15 Nov | 16–22 Nov | **~22 Nov** |
| **P2** session kit | Wk 10 · 16–21 Nov | 23–29 Nov | 30 Nov – 6 Dec | **~6 Dec** |
| **P1** Fiber metering | Wk 12 · 30 Nov – 5 Dec | 7–13 Dec | 14–20 Dec | **~20 Dec** |

**Why P1 goes last.** Two reasons. A `[DIS]` plus a `[VOT]` is fourteen days minimum,
so nothing posted in Week 12 can resolve before the programme closes on 5 December —
P1's campaign was always going to run late. And going last means P3 has resolved
(~22 Nov) and P2 has resolved (~6 Dec) before the conversion interview, so I arrive
with **two settled community votes** rather than one still in progress.

**Ask small.** The quorum is **3× the requested amount**, so the size of the ask sets
the difficulty directly. A modest ask needs modest turnout. Three passed votes are
worth far more than one ambitious vote that failed to reach quorum.

## Application templates

### Spark — sections required

1. Project overview
2. Team profile
3. Background — the problem
4. Solution
5. Technical approach
6. Execution plan
7. Funding breakdown
8. Deliverables, **with how each one can be verified**
9. Current state vs. what the funding would add
10. Alignment with the CKB ecosystem

Judged on: alignment with Web5 principles · technical feasibility and innovation ·
whether the testing and operating plan is realistic · team capability · potential
community value.

### Community Fund DAO `[DIS]` — sections required

1. **Why?** — why this should exist
2. **Why you?** — why I am the person to build it
3. **Why now?**
4. **Budget** — the amount, and the justification for it
5. **How?** — the implementation plan, with milestones

## The thing that actually decides this

**The 30-like gate is a reputation gate, not a writing gate.** A well-written
proposal from an account nobody recognises does not get 30 likes in seven days. An
average proposal from someone who has spent twelve weeks filing issues, publishing
findings and answering other people's questions does.

That is why block D of every single day is a public action, and why the daily forum
reply is treated as load-bearing work rather than as a nicety. The campaign does not
start in November. It starts on 16 September.

**Running tally** — updated every Saturday:

| | Count |
|---|---|
| Forum posts written | 0 |
| Forum replies to other people | 0 |
| Issues / PRs filed in other repositories | 0 |
| Likes received on my own posts | 0 |

**If P3's `[DIS]` does not reach 30 likes by day 7:** do not immediately repost it.
That burns the same audience twice. Pull it, publish the Spark completion report
instead, and let P2's campaign be the first vote — by then there are four more weeks
of reputation behind it.
